# MekongPulse — Competitive landscape: digital flood early warning + agronomic advisory

Researched 18 Oct 2026. Every fact carries a source ID [S#]; all URLs and access dates are in Sources.
Statements marked **Inference:** are my reasoning, not sourced facts. All sources accessed 18 Oct 2026.

## 1. Products by audience and channel

### 1.1 Hydrological forecast layers — agency/expert-facing, web-first
- **Google Flood Hub** covers river basins in 150+ countries and lists **Cambodia** among covered territories; forecasts run up to 7 days, are free, and update daily [S1][S2]. Scope is **riverine floods only** (no urban flood maps), and Google labels output "informational purposes only… not a substitute for data from government agencies" [S3].
- Flood Hub's own delivery channels: alerts surfaced in **Google Search, Google Maps and Android notifications "in conjunction with authoritative resources"**, plus an **email subscription** (requires a logged-in Google account) that sends country-level summaries of significant events [S2][S4]. Audience is named as "governments, aid organizations, and individuals" [S5].
- A separate **"expert mode" data layer** (~250,000 forecast points via "virtual gauges", 150 countries) is explicitly intended for hydrologists, experts and organisations doing analysis [S5][S6].
- **Documented criticism:** Li et al. (2026, *Journal of Hydrology X*) reassess the system and find "the reported accuracy of Google's AI flood model is substantially overstated when evaluated against real observations", with ">90% rates of false positives and false negatives", warning it "risks misinforming those who depend on its outputs… particularly in less-developed countries" [S7]. A separate 2026 skill assessment benchmarks Flood Hub (with GloFAS) against discharge observations in Mali for early action [S8]. Practical limits: it needs an internet connection, the forecasting API is restricted to pilot participants, and offline alerting is handled through partner organisations [S6].
- **MRC (Mekong River Commission):** RFDMC in Phnom Penh takes 138 hydro-met stations and predicts 22 mainstream forecast points during June–October; bulletins go to **National Mekong Committees, national forecasting agencies, CSOs and media, who re-disseminate** to the public; flash-flood guidance is issued twice daily [S9][S10]. The **Situation Room** is a centralised decision-support database linked to the RFDMC [S11].
- MRC's public-facing layer is the **One Mekong app** (launched Jan 2025): real-time monitoring/forecasting data, push notifications for flood/drought/weather/disaster, an "iReport" community incident feed and a ChatGPT-based chatbot ("Nong Nam") [S11][S12]. MRC also runs a **weekly flood/drought forecast TV channel on Facebook/YouTube, broadcast in English with subtitles in Mekong languages** [S13]. No MRC-operated Telegram channel was found; its distribution to citizens is still via NMCs/agencies/media [S9][S13].
- **One Mekong River Watch** (mekongflow.org, independent, free) is the closest existing analogue to a public river dashboard: it "refurbishes MRC telemetry for fast mobile use", shows MRC thresholds/status, flags stale/no-data stations, offers **opt-in community alerts**, and states it "does not replace official emergency instructions" [S14].
  **Inference:** its honest-limitations framing and MRC-source attribution are the trust pattern a new entrant should copy; it has no crop, stage or agronomic layer.
- **Cambodia government channels:** NCDM is the coordinating authority; the **National Roadmap for Early Warnings for All 2025–2028** builds on four pillars (risk knowledge; observation & forecasting; **warning dissemination & communication**; preparedness and response), notes ~80% of Cambodians live in rural, climate-sensitive locations and ~65% depend on agriculture, and gives WFP/NCDM the job of a multi-stakeholder coordination platform with last-mile communication to women, indigenous peoples and persons with disabilities [S15].
- **MOWRAM/FAO PEARL project** (GCF-funded, US$43m, 6 years, northern Tonle Sap) introduced a **monthly agrometeorological bulletin distributed via Telegram, Facebook and other social media**, with rainfall/sunlight/heat-accumulation data and crop-specific advice (cashew, organic rice, mango, leafy vegetables); target 450,000 farmers [S16].

### 1.2 National last-mile warning — citizen-facing, IVR/voice/loudspeaker
- **EWS 1294** (People in Need + NCDM, national since 2020): users register by **calling 1294 and choosing province/district/commune**; provincial authorities (PCDM) then push **pre-recorded or self-recorded voice calls (IVR)** to subscribers in the affected location; subscription is free on Smart, Cellcard and Metfone [S17][S18].
- Scale: ~**190,000 registered users**, 41 sensor locations, "over 1.1 million people benefited" (Jan 2024) [S19]; 774,400+ alerts to 120,000 users 2014–2022 [S20]; +63,000 users onboarded in the 2024–25 ActionAid/GSMA upgrade [S21]. Channels now include **IVR calls, public loudspeakers, Telegram groups, radio and SMS** [S22].
- Documented limitations: reach depends on subscription, and **people changing SIM cards lose the service**; awareness is low (most surveyed vulnerable-group respondents did not know the system existed); PIN itself recommends moving to **broadcast-based dissemination** (cell broadcast), which is now being trialled in Cambodia [S17][S20][S21][S22]. Historic baseline from the 2010 floods: 36% of Cambodians received no risk information and 72% of those warned received it too late [S20].
- Cell broadcast is the direction of travel regionally: it needs no registration and no internet, and UNDRR cites Cambodia's move toward broader SMS broadcasting with telecom partners [S22].

### 1.3 Anticipatory action / forecast-based financing
- WFP's AA programme delivered forecast-triggered cash to **1.2 million people in 2025**; early-warning messaging is the other half of the intervention package [S24][S25]. **WFP Cambodia stated it would prioritise a cash approach with early alerts through Cambodia's official early warning systems** for floods (target date April 2024) [S26]. FAO's AA package similarly pairs cash/feed with "early warning messages, seasonal forecasts, and livestock management advisories" [S27]. CERF/OCHA frameworks pre-arrange finance against agreed triggers [S28].
- Evidence that *agronomy-relevant* warnings change outcomes in comparable settings: in Bangladesh's haor wetlands, "local farmers could save their rice crop after receiving forecast and early warning (voice message)" during the 2020 floods [S29]; haor boro rice is lost when pre-monsoon floods arrive before harvest [S30]; and in Khulna/Jessore, fishers "used early flood warnings to harvest ahead of disasters, securing profits of up to BDT 200,000" [S31].
- Bangladesh's Deltares/RIMES "TamTam" project used **voice message broadcast** (union-level, 5-day forecast, 300 messages/day) with FFWC/BWDB backing; post-season evaluation found communities "received, understood and trusted the information… and took actions" [S32]. RCT evidence shows anticipatory cash improves food security and protects assets versus post-flood aid [S33].

### 1.4 Crop-stage advisory tools — how stage/planting input is captured
- **IRRI Rice Crop Manager (RCM)** asks the operator for field location, **crop variety, planting date, crop establishment method, water source/status and harvest method**, then returns a one-page printout or SMS with fertilizer products, amounts and timings; it is designed for use by **extension workers, crop advisors and service providers** on smartphones/computers, with offline and pre-printed versions for weak connectivity [S34][S35]. >4M recommendations across Bangladesh, India, Indonesia, Philippines, Vietnam; adoption is gradual; IRRI's stated next steps include integrating weather forecasts and delivering advice through **LLM voice/chat interfaces in local languages** [S34]. Philippines RCMAS reached 1.4M farmers / 2.7M recommendations, but only 27% of surveyed farmers had received and applied a recommendation [S36].
- **AfricaRice RiceAdvice**: free, offline Android app; a **5–10 minute interview** about the rice-growing environment, crop management and current yield level produces fertilizer choice, amounts and application timing at a chosen target yield; 60,000+ advices; the stated scalability constraint is that **many farmers do not own a smartphone**, so advice is often generated by service providers [S37].
- **AKILIMO (IITA)**: Android app plus printable advisory sheets for extension workers, generating site-specific guidance on fertilizer, intercropping and scheduled planting from farm characteristics and management choices [S38].
- **Digital Green Farmer.Chat**: local-language generative-AI assistant (app/web) accepting **voice, text and photos**; deployed with 4,500+ extension agents across Kenya and India (as of Jan 2024) [S39][S40].
- **Plantix**: photo-based diagnosis of pests, diseases and nutrient deficiencies; company claims 100M+ questions answered and ~90% diagnostic accuracy (vs 60–70% for human experts per a GSMA interview) [S41][S42]. Image-first; no planting-date or crop-stage input.
- **ChivCheat (Cambodia, cashew)**: **Khmer-language Telegram chatbot** accepting images, voice messages or text, built by a CGIAR/Impact Hub-supported startup on Google's Gemini API; CGIAR reports **60% of farmers in the pilot area (Kampong Thom) already used Telegram** [S43].
- **India IMD agromet advisories**: district-level advisories are prepared by Agromet Field Units (officials/extension) and pushed to farmers by SMS and IVR — PIB reports ~6.16M farmers served under a PPP channel [S44], and an earlier CCAFS study reports SMS reaching 2.5M farmers across 16 states with IVR voicemail reaching 30,000 [S45]. Voice-agent advisory trials for smallholders are also reported in India [S46].

## 2. Answers to the key questions

**Q1 — Do dashboards serve officials while farmers get SMS/IVR/voice? Yes; the split is consistent across every system surveyed.**
- MRC's daily bulletins and Situation Room target National Mekong Committees, line agencies, NGOs and media, which then re-disseminate; only the One Mekong app addresses the general public directly [S9][S10][S11].
- EWS 1294 works exactly this way: officials operate the platform and choose the target location; citizens receive IVR voice calls, loudspeakers, radio, SMS and Telegram posts [S17][S18][S22].
- Google Flood Hub keeps an "expert mode" layer for hydrologists and an email subscription for country-level watchers, while general alerts arrive via Search/Maps/Android notifications [S4][S5][S6].
- Bangladesh: FFWC/BWDB officials hold the forecast, while flood-prone unions receive localised voice messages [S32]. India: Agromet Field Units prepare district advisories for the extension system, farmers get SMS/IVR [S44][S45].
- **Inference:** in Cambodia the dashboard-shaped surface is an inter-agency coordination artefact; farmer value is created only by the voice/radio/loudspeaker/SMS leg.

**Q2 — Known failure modes for farmer-facing tools in rural Cambodia/SEA.** (a) Subscription and SIM churn break reach; broadcast (cell broadcast) is recommended instead [S18][S20][S21]. (b) Awareness of existing services is low, and marginalised users rely on a trusted human ("I only receive information when the village chief comes to tell me") [S20][S21]. (c) Literacy and digital literacy require **voice** over text; EWS 1294's IVR exists for that reason and ActionAid promoted it explicitly [S21][S22]. (d) High volumes of spam SMS make source trust decisive [S21]; over-alerting is a recognised failure mode in comparable national systems [S22]. (e) Connectivity and device cost: internet penetration was 60.7% in Cambodia at the start of 2025, so roughly a third of the population is not reachable online [S47], and agri-advisory practitioners report poor signal and limited devices as reasons to ship offline versions and paper templates [S34][S37]. (f) Language: MRC's own public channel is English with subtitles [S13], while Khmer-native chat (ChivCheat) shows demand when the interface is in Khmer [S43]. (g) The academic review of digital extension services in Cambodia, the Philippines and Vietnam frames these as structural barriers and enablers, not adoption failures to be blamed on users [S48].

**Q3 — How stage-based systems get the stage input.** The dominant pattern is **one structured interview per season, at or near planting, converted into a season-long schedule**, not a recurring stage question: RCM captures planting date, establishment method, variety and water status once and returns timed fertilizer advice [S34][S35]; RiceAdvice runs a 5–10 minute interview and outputs application timing for the season [S37]; AKILIMO works the same way and additionally ships printable sheets for extension workers [S38]. Multiple crops/seasons mean the interview is repeated for the next crop [S36 notes start/end-of-season interviews]. Conversational and image-based tools (Farmer.Chat, Plantix, ChivCheat) do not maintain a crop calendar at all [S39][S40][S41][S43]. Flood warning products contain no crop-stage input whatsoever [S3][S9][S17].

**Q4 — Gaps.** (a) **Nothing surveyed joins a river-flood forecast to crop-stage-specific field directives in Cambodia**: MRC/EWS 1294/Flood Hub stop at hazard; RCM/RiceAdvice/AKILIMO stop at agronomy with no hazard trigger; ChivCheat covers cashew disease only [S3][S9][S17][S34][S37][S43]. (b) **Spoken Khmer delivery of agronomic instructions does not exist as a product**: EWS 1294's IVR is generic pre-recorded safety messaging [S17][S18]; the FAO/PEARL bulletin is text on Telegram/Facebook [S16]; MRC's chatbot and TV are text/English [S12][S13]. (c) **Per-farmer stage personalisation is absent**: no flood system knows what is in the field, and RCM/RiceAdvice collect stage data through an intermediary rather than per user on a messaging channel [S34][S37]. (d) Granularity: MRC gives 22 mainstream forecast points and Flood Hub gives gauge points — neither maps to commune-level field risk [S3][S9]. (e) Bangladesh shows the payoff if (a)–(c) are solved: voice warnings let farmers harvest or save rice before the flood [S29][S31].

## 3. Implications for MekongPulse

- **Where per-farmer crop-stage selection should live: in the chat/voice channel (Telegram), asked once per season, not at alert time.** Every credible stage model in the survey collects the input once per crop and derives stage from planting date thereafter [S34][S37]. Asking a low-literacy user to classify their crop stage during an emergency contradicts the documented voice-first requirement [S21][S22]. Practical shape: a 3-option prompt in Khmer ("just planted / growing / almost harvest"), stored per Telegram user alongside their commune, with voice-reply support, an obvious "change stage" command, and a commune-default calendar for users who never answer. Bulk-setting by CDMC/extension staff is the fallback the literature supports, since RCM-type tools are operated by intermediaries [S34][S36].
- **Web dashboard audience and job: officials and coordinators — commune chiefs/CDMC, PCDM/NCDM, district agriculture offices, NGO/anticipatory-action programme staff — for situational awareness, prioritisation and verification, not for farmers.** This mirrors MRC's Situation Room and EWS 1294's operator dashboard, where the platform's user is the agency that then pushes voice/SMS downstream [S11][S17][S22]. Concretely it should answer: which communes are escalating, on what station evidence, what directive was sent, to how many users, and where coverage is thin (the "measure registered users per location" need raised by Cambodian commune leaders [S21]). A public read-only view with visible data sources and thresholds (mekongflow.org's honesty pattern [S14]) doubles as the trust artifact for journalists and aid organisations, and as the hook into WFP/FAO anticipatory-action workflows that already route alerts through Cambodia's official systems [S26][S27].
- **Evidence to lean on in the pitch:** Telegram is already used by Cambodian farmers where a Khmer bot exists (60% in the ChivCheat pilot area [S43]) and is already an EWS 1294 dissemination channel [S22][S19]; voice warnings demonstrably change crop-saving behaviour in comparable flood systems [S29][S31][S32]; and IRRI itself names LLM voice/chat in local languages plus weather-forecast integration as RCM's next direction, i.e. no incumbent owns this space yet [S34].
- **Do not build:** an SMS/IVR/cell-broadcast stack (NCDM/PIN/GSMA own that, and it is mid-migration [S21][S22]); a hazard-only dashboard (MRC and Flood Hub already publish that raw layer [S9][S14]); or a general agronomy chatbot (RCM/RiceAdvice/Farmer.Chat own it [S34][S37][S39]). Inference: the defensible slot is hazard × stage × spoken Khmer, delivered on channels farmers already use.
