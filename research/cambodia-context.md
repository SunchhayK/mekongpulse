# Cambodia context and fact-check

Verification of figures and context used by `product-report.md` (MekongPulse). One section per checked item: claim, verdict, correct figure, sources. All URLs below accessed 2026-10-18. Verdicts: **verified** (figure confirmed), **corrected** (number or name wrong), **unverified** (no institutional source found).

## 1. 2022 flood damage and per-hectare losses (report lines 21, 101)

**Claim.** "In 2022, 140,000 ha of Cambodian farmland submerged; smallholders lost $400-$700/ha in sunk fertilizer and seed costs."

**Verdict: corrected.** Area is approximately right but should cite the official figure; the money figure is not supported as "fertilizer and seed."

**Correct figure.** NCDM data via HRF SitRep No. 2 (mid-event, 1 Sep - 11 Oct 2022): 85,482 households affected in 14 provinces, 15 people killed, 33,165 houses affected, 4,498 households evacuated, **152,386 ha of agricultural land affected**. Use 152,386 ha (all agricultural land, not only rice; mid-event, season not over). No source found for "$400-$700/ha fertilizer and seed"; closest verified cost is total rice production cost US$405-500/ha (CFAP). If the loss figure stays, label it as value at risk per hectare, not input costs.

**Sources.**
- HRF SitRep No. 2: https://reliefweb.int/report/cambodia/floods-cambodia-2022-situation-report-sitrep-no-2-12-october-2022-humanitarian-response-forum-hrf
- CFAP cost data via Asia Pacific Farmers Forum: https://asiapacificfarmersforum.net/rice-farming-in-cambodia/

## 2. EWS1294 reach and content (report lines 22, 29)

**Claim.** EWS1294 gives "generic evacuation warnings" only ("Move cattle to high ground"), with zero agronomic guidance.

**Verdict: corrected.** Not evacuation-only; the "no agronomic guidance" part holds. A "500,000 registered users" figure circulating in drafts is **unverified** for EWS1294.

**Correct figure.** 118,351 unique subscribers (2 Nov 2021); >774,400 alerts sent to ~120,000 registered users; operated with NCDM; free; subscribe by calling 1294. Also sends storm, extreme-UV and COVID-19 messages plus preparedness instructions. Survey: 100% of respondents received messages, 88% highly satisfied, almost all took protective action (28% moved valuables, 24% stocked supplies, 21% moved family). The "more than 500,000 users" line in FHI360's digital-resilience compendium belongs to eKrishok (Bangladesh): do not cite it for EWS1294. The exact quoted warning "Move cattle to high ground" was not found in any source; paraphrase sourced content instead.

**Sources.**
- PIN brochure: https://resources.peopleinneed.net/documents/1055-1.pdf
- PIN impact assessment: https://cambodia.peopleinneed.net/media/publications/1867/file/impact-assesment-on-ews-1294-reach.pdf
- PIN programme page: https://cambodia.peopleinneed.net/en/cambodias-early-warning-system-1294-an-adaptable-technology-promoting-safety-for-all-9146gp

## 4. Fertilizer economics and "apply 24 h before flood" (report line 22)

**Claim.** Farmers "apply $150/ha chemical fertilizer 24 hours before river flood surges".

**Verdict: unverified** (the behaviour); **$150/ha is plausible but not directly sourced.**

**Correct figure.** Applied fertilizer in rainfed Cambodian paddy: up to 163 kg/ha N+P2O5+K2O; best gross return above fertilizer cost near 120 kg/ha (Srun Khema et al. 2022). Recommended rates: N 80-120, P2O5 30-60, K2O 30-60 kg/ha (Baron Point 2025). Cambodian 2024 prices: urea $380-450/t, NPK $450-550/t, DAP $520-600/t, MOP $350-420/t. Applying recommended rates costs roughly US$120-240/ha, so $150/ha is a defensible single-application approximation; present it as approximate or drop it. No source documents applying fertilizer ~24 h before a flood: remove the timing.

**Sources.**
- Srun Khema et al. 2022, Sustainability 14(17): 10708: https://doi.org/10.3390/su141710708 (paper abstract; MDPI full text/PDF blocked from this network)
- Baron Point, The Fertilizer Industry in Cambodia (Jul 2025): https://www.baronpoint.com/public/uploads/white-papers/202507-Fertilizer_Baron_Point_Institutional_Markets_Report-01-01.pdf

## 5. Land titles as loan collateral ("Bann Dei") (report line 23)

**Claim.** Smallholders pledge land titles for inputs; flood crop losses cause default, poverty, migration.

**Verdict: verified** in substance. Naming caveat: the source literature says "land title as collateral" / "hard or soft title"; no source uses the romanized term "Bann Dei", keep it only as a Khmer gloss.

**Correct figure.** INEF/Bliss 2022: a land title corresponds to a loan of about US$4,729; US$10,111 paid out per ha of land collateral; national "micro" loan average >US$4,200; main loans average US$5,183 (median US$3,500); poor-borrower sample mean US$2,172 (median US$1,000). UCL/Guermond et al. 2022: six MFIs (ACLEDA, Hattha, Sathapana, Amret, LOLC, PRASAC) hold ~75% of microloans; 28% of indebted households have used agricultural or residential land as collateral; over-indebtedness undermines climate adaptation. PRASAC: 99.9% of loans secured by land title. HRW "Debt Traps" (Sep 2025) documents predatory collateral practices.

**Sources.**
- INEF/Bliss 2022: https://www.licadho-cambodia.org/reports/files/INEFReport-MicrofinanceInCambodia2022.pdf
- UCL/Guermond et al. 2022: https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10157957/1/
- Pulitzer Center (PRASAC 99.9%): https://pulitzercenter.org/stories/cambodias-indigenous-communities-renounce-communal-land-titles-microloans
- HRW: https://www.hrw.org/report/2025/09/24/debt-traps/predatory-microfinance-loans-and-exploitation-of-cambodias-indigenous

## 6. Rice calendar (report lines 53-54, 188, 321)

**Claim basis.** Commune calendar with a "ripening" default; stage directives (tillering/vegetative vs ripening) for Kandal, Kampong Cham, Prey Veng, Battambang.

**Verdict: verified** at season level; month-by-stage tables for these provinces were not found in institutional sources.

**Correct figure.** Wet season (~70% of annual output, FAO GIEWS): sow May-Jun; harvest Aug-Sep for short/medium-duration varieties, Oct-Jan for longer-duration. Dry season: sow Nov-Dec; harvest Feb-Apr (USDA GAIN CB2025-0006, Dec 2025). Battambang rainfed lowlands face a shifting planting window under climate change; traditional broadcasting uses ~300 kg seed/ha (NURTURE). Product note: a fixed "ripening" default fits only the Oct-Jan harvest window; from Jun-Sep the wet-season crop is mostly vegetative/reproductive, so make the default season-aware.

**Sources.**
- USDA GAIN CB2025-0006: https://apps.fas.usda.gov/newgainapi/api/Report/DownloadReportByFileName?fileName=Grain%20and%20Feed%20Update_Phnom%20Penh_Cambodia_CB2025-0006
- FAO GIEWS country brief (2 Feb 2026): https://www.fao.org/giews/countrybrief/country.jsp?code=KHM
- NURTURE project, mechanised planting in Battambang: https://nurtureproject.org/achievement/downloads/3.%20Mechanised%20Planting%20in%20BAT.pdf

## 7. Flood seasonality and warning levels (context for lines 21, 149)

**Verdict: verified.** MRC defines the flood (wet) season as June-October, dry season November-May. In 2022 the monsoon onset was announced 18 June; flood peaks exceeded alarm levels at Stung Treng (1 Oct, 9.85 m), Kratie (2 Oct, 20.54 m), Kompong Cham (5 Oct, 13.81 m); tropical storms NORU and MULAN kept Oct-Dec levels above long-term averages.

**Correct figure (alarm/flood levels, m, MRC FFW bulletin).** Prek Kdam 9.50/10.00; Phnom Penh (Bassac) 10.50/12.00; Phnom Penh Port 9.50/11.00; Koh Khel 7.90/8.40; Neak Luong 7.50/8.00. As of 16 Oct 2026 no flood warning was in place; Prek Kdam stood at ~6.3 m (~3.2 m below alarm), consistent with the report's ">2.0 m below alarm" scenario.

**Sources.**
- MRC flood and drought forecasting: https://www.mrcmekong.org/flood-and-drought-forecasting/
- MRC FFW daily bulletin: https://ffw.mrcmekong.org/bulletin_wet.php
- MRC Seasonal Flood Situation Report 2022: https://ffw.mrcmekong.org/weekly_report/2023/Seasonal%20Flood%20Situation%20Report%20RFFS%202022.pdf

## 8. "Koh Thom" and "Peam Chor" administrative units (report lines 42, 136, 163, 224, 293, 320, 373)

**Claim basis.** Both names are treated as communes (personas, early-adopters, test scenarios).

**Verdict: corrected.** Both are districts; neither has a commune of the same name.

**Correct figure.**
- Kaoh Thum ("Koh Thom") district, Kandal (0804): 11 communes; `080402` is Chrouy Ta Kaev, not "Koh Thom"; the namesake communes are `080404` Kaoh Thum Ka and `080405` Kaoh Thum Kha. District population 129,045 (2019).
- Peam Chor district, Prey Veng (1406): 10 communes (140601 Angkor Angk to 140610 Svay Phluoh), none named "Peam Chor". River-bank communes suitable for the persona: e.g. `140605` Kaoh Sampov, `140607` Preaek Krabau, `140603` Kaoh Chek, `140604` Kaoh Roka. District population 61,454 (2019).

**Sources.**
- Commune lists: https://www.stat.go.jp/info/meetings/cambodia/pdf/08com_rd.pdf (Kandal), https://www.stat.go.jp/info/meetings/cambodia/pdf/14com_rd.pdf (Prey Veng)
- Populations: https://www.citypopulation.de/en/cambodia/admin/

## 9. NCDM flood statistics (context)

**Verdict: verified** for the headline figures; larger circulating numbers unverified. 2022: use section 1 (NCDM via HRF; no end-of-season consolidated NCDM total located). 2023: NCDM said impact was "less severe than in 2022"; as of 3 Oct 2023, 9,196 families and 7,199 ha of paddy were affected nationally, with >15,000 houses at risk in seven provinces. Larger 2023 figures in foreign-language media (36,860 households; 33,440 ha rice damaged; 30,250 ha yield reduced) could not be tied to a source year: **unverified, do not cite**.

**Source.** Cambodianess: https://cambodianess.com/article/more-than-15000-homes-hit-by-floods
